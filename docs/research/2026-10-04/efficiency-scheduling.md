# Efficient sync and durable scheduling

Research checked 4 October 2026. Proposal only; backend standards remain unapproved. Scope: ten private users, 600 recipient deliveries/user/day maximum and 30/mailbox/day, approved campaigns running while PCs are off. No implementation, deployment or empirical benchmark.

## Recommended bounded algorithm

1. Authenticate incoming Gmail/Pub/Sub events, map the registered connection to its owner, and atomically persist a deduplicated receipt plus mailbox dirty generation/outbox entry before acknowledging. Collapse repeated notifications into one outstanding sync job; store history IDs as decimal strings, not JavaScript numbers. Notifications identify changes, not complete messages. Renew watches daily with staggered jitter; persist returned expiry and alert before expiration. Google requires renewal at least every seven days and warns that notifications can drop. [Gmail push](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/push)
2. Claim one sync lease per mailbox. Read history from the committed cursor, fetch only changed metadata and requested bodies, then commit upserts/checkpoint. Bound each turn to a proposed two pages or five seconds; persist continuation and requeue. After the final page, clear dirty only if its generation has not advanced during processing; otherwise continue. HTTP 404 requires resumable full resync, with follow-ups paused and historical alerts suppressed. Refresh last-successful-sync even for empty results. [Gmail sync](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/sync)
3. Proposed idle reconciliation: hourly, staggered by mailbox. Active follow-up mailboxes retain five-minute reconciliation/freshness checks; stale sync blocks admission. Push cannot prove absence of replies. Before a newly activated idle mailbox sends follow-ups, reconcile it. Never narrow watched labels so that archived replies disappear from campaign detection.
4. One cloud minute Cron tick selects bounded indexed due work: approved send steps, sync/renewal deadlines, unpublished outbox entries and expired leases. Store UTC due instants and original IANA timezone; resolve DST on approval. Claim conditionally and commit an outbox ID with the state transition. Relay IDs only; mark publication afterward. Crash after publishing produces duplicates, resolved against D1 state. Cron is UTC; queue delays are limited to 24 hours, so long schedules belong in D1, not a queue alone. [Cron](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/cron-triggers/), [Queue delays](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/batching-retries/), [D1 API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/worker-api/d1-database/)
5. Dispatch rechecks owner, approval/version, suppression, recipient state, freshness and rolling quotas atomically. Conditional zero-row transitions must prevent dependent writes; inspect results. Persist submitting before calling provider. Expired submitting leases become unknown; fencing cannot cancel an accepted external request. Reconcile provider evidence, never blindly replay. Queue delivery is at least once, not exactly-once sending. [Delivery guarantees](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/reference/delivery-guarantees/)

## Fairness, overload and recovery

Rotate across ten users, then mailboxes, with one concurrent task/mailbox and bounded global provider concurrency. Prioritise suppression ingestion and unknown-send recovery; reserve capacity for due dispatch/sync, defer imports/backfills. Persist capped exponential backoff with jitter and provider Retry-After. Retry safe reads/publications; ambiguous sends follow the unknown path. Acknowledge individual completed queue messages to avoid replaying successful batch peers. Dead-letter exhaustion alerts and retains recoverable database work; the tick republishes eligible stalled jobs without permitting a second submission. [Queue retries](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/batching-retries/)

## Arithmetic and decision gate

Thirty-day, 200-mailbox ceiling scenario: five-minute polling creates 200 × 288 × 30 = **1,728,000 sync jobs**. Hourly idle reconciliation creates **144,000**, plus 6,000 daily watch renewals and unknown push-driven jobs P. With 180,000 send jobs, three operations/job gives 5,724,000 versus 972,000 + 3P operations, excluding renewals queued separately, retries, receipts and other work. Batching reduces invocations, not per-message Queue operations. Paid allowance is one million/month, then US$0.40/million; baseline polling overage is approximately US$1.89. [Queue pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/pricing/)

This saving applies only to idle mailboxes: 200 continuously active follow-up mailboxes retain the polling floor. Inbound volume, label churn, API pagination and P are unknown; 30 outgoing/day does not bound them. Under A$15 remains unproven. Measure CPU, rows scanned/written, push-to-sync ratio, backlog age and total USD/FX/tax costs before approving capacity; never save money by weakening suppression or freshness.
